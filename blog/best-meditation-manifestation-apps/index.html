The best meditation and manifestation apps for 2026.

The health app space is loud for a category about quiet. Here is the short, honest map: the best overall, the best quiet timer, the best for privacy, where a voice or a free library still wins, and the one manifestation method with evidence.

4 minute read

Disclosure: we make Luvia, so read this as a maker's list. We still name the two places where another app beats us outright, and who should pick them instead.

Two categories get conflated every January: meditation apps, which help you sit with what is already here, and manifestation apps, which promise to bring something new. The first has decades of practice behind it and a crowded, high-quality market. The second is mostly vision boards and affirmations, and it has a problem: the research suggests pure positive visualisation can reduce the effort people put in. So this guide judges each category by what actually matters in it: for meditation, whether the app gets out of the way; for manifestation, whether the method has evidence.

Best overall: Luvia

Luvia wins the top spot because it is the only app here that covers both halves of the title well. The meditation timer is deliberately small: a session length, optional opening and closing bells, and nothing to configure or scroll. The manifestation side is not a vision board but WOOP, the four-step method with twenty years of studies behind it, guided inside the app's rituals. Around both sit a mood tracker, a private journal and notes to your future self, all kept on your device, with no ads and no tracking. And Kind Messages adds the thing no meditation app has: a moment of anonymous human warmth after you sit. Skip it if you want guided courses, a free app or Android; it is subscription only, and the subscription is what pays for moderation instead of advertising.

Best guided meditation: Headspace and Calm

If you want to be taught, these two remain the reference points, and here they beat Luvia. Headspace is the friendlier teacher, with structured courses that genuinely build a practice from zero. Calm leans toward atmosphere, sleep stories and soundscapes. Both are polished, both are subscriptions, and both are content businesses, which means notifications, seasonal campaigns and a home screen that wants your attention. Pick one if a voice helps you stay. Many people graduate from them the day the talking stops helping, and that is the day Luvia's silent timer starts to make sense.

Best free library: Insight Timer

Insight Timer's free library is enormous: tens of thousands of guided sessions, teachers from every tradition, and a good plain timer inside it. On sheer free volume nothing else comes close, including us. The cost is choice overload and a busy, social interface, with profiles, milestones and community features wrapped around the sitting. If browsing teachers energises you, it is the best value in the category. If the browsing becomes the practice, that is the moment for something smaller.

Best quiet timer: Luvia

A meditation timer should be the least interesting screen on your phone, and Luvia's is. Two taps to start, bells you can switch off, no streak to protect, no library to feel behind on, no notification asking you back. Insight Timer's timer is good but sits inside a social feed; Headspace and Calm bury theirs under the courses. If what you want from an app is to be left alone for ten minutes, Luvia does that best.

Best for privacy: Luvia

Meditation apps collect surprisingly intimate data: when you cannot sleep, when you are stressed, what you searched for at 3 a.m. Luvia keeps your sessions, mood and journal on the device and has no advertising business to feed. The big subscription apps are not careless, but they are content companies with analytics and marketing stacks, and their privacy policies read that way. We put ours in plain words in Is Luvia safe?

Best manifestation app: Luvia, with WOOP

Most manifestation apps are affirmation feeds and collage tools, and they inherit the visualisation problem: imagining the outcome vividly, on its own, tends to relax the drive to pursue it. The exception is WOOP, the method built from Gabriele Oettingen's research on mental contrasting: picture the wish and its best outcome, then face the obstacle inside you, then decide in advance what you will do when it appears. Luvia guides all four steps, keeps your woops together, and sits them next to the journal where you will notice whether the plan held. The free official app at woopmylife.org is the honest alternative if you only want WOOP and nothing else. We wrote the full story, with the studies, in WOOP: the manifestation method with science behind it.

How to choose

Want to be taught, with structure and a voice? Headspace, or Calm if atmosphere matters more than curriculum. Want maximum free material and enjoy exploring? Insight Timer. For everyone else, the quiet timer, the evidence-backed manifestation method, the privacy and the kindness live in one app, and that app is Luvia. Whichever you pick for sitting still, give manifestation five minutes of WOOP before you give it a vision board.
