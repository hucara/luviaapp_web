The best meditation and manifestation apps for 2026.

The health app space is loud for a category about quiet. Here is the short, honest map: who guided meditation is for, where the free depth is, and which manifestation method actually has evidence.

4 minute read

Disclosure: we make Luvia, and it appears below. The other entries are genuine recommendations, and we say plainly who should pick them instead of us.

Two categories get conflated every January: meditation apps, which help you sit with what is already here, and manifestation apps, which promise to bring something new. The first has decades of practice behind it and a crowded, high-quality market. The second is mostly vision boards and affirmations, and it has a problem: the research suggests pure positive visualisation can reduce the effort people put in. So this guide treats them differently: for meditation, it is about matching a style to you; for manifestation, it is about picking the one method with evidence.

Best guided meditation: Headspace and Calm

If you want to be taught, these two remain the reference points. Headspace is the friendlier teacher, with structured courses that genuinely build a practice from zero. Calm leans toward atmosphere, sleep stories and soundscapes. Both are polished, both are subscriptions, and both are content businesses, which means notifications, seasonal campaigns and a home screen that wants your attention. Pick one if a voice helps you stay. Where Luvia differs: no voice, no course, no content to keep up with, just a quiet timer with bells, for people whose meditation improved the day the talking stopped.

Best free depth: Insight Timer

Insight Timer's free library is enormous: tens of thousands of guided sessions, teachers from every tradition, and a genuinely good plain timer inside it. The cost is choice overload and a busy, social interface, with profiles, milestones and community features wrapped around the sitting. If browsing teachers energises you, it is the best value in the category. If the browsing becomes the practice, that is the moment for something smaller; Luvia's timer starts in two taps and asks nothing of you afterwards.

Manifestation: skip the vision boards, use WOOP

Most manifestation apps are affirmation feeds and collage tools, and they inherit the visualisation problem: imagining the outcome vividly, on its own, tends to relax the drive to pursue it. The exception is WOOP, the method built from twenty years of research on mental contrasting: picture the wish and its best outcome, then face the obstacle inside you, then decide in advance what you will do when it appears. The free official app at woopmylife.org teaches it well, and Luvia builds the same four steps into its rituals, guided and kept alongside your journal. We wrote the full story, with the studies, in WOOP: the manifestation method with science behind it.

How to choose

Want to be taught, with structure and a voice? Headspace, or Calm if atmosphere matters more than curriculum. Want maximum free material and enjoy exploring? Insight Timer. Want quiet, privacy, and one app that also holds your mood, journal and goals? That is Luvia. And whichever you pick for sitting still, give manifestation five evidence-backed minutes with WOOP before you give it a vision board.
