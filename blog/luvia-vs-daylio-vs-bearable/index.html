Luvia vs Daylio vs Bearable: which fits you?

Three good apps that want different things for you. The right one depends on whether you are optimising for speed, for health data, or for calm.

4 minute read

Honesty first: Luvia is our app, so read this as a maker's comparison rather than a neutral review. We have tried to be fair about what the others do better, because they genuinely do some things better.

Mood trackers look interchangeable from the outside. A row of faces, a chart, a streak. Underneath, they are built around very different beliefs about what tracking is for, and picking against the grain of your own need is the main reason people quit after a week. Here is where each of these three actually shines.

Daylio: the fastest log there is

Daylio's whole design is speed. Two taps record a mood and a set of activities, widgets keep it on your home screen, and streaks plus stats reward consistency. If your goal is a long, unbroken record with the least possible friction, and you enjoy seeing correlations between activities and mood emerge from months of data, it is excellent, and its free tier makes it the easiest to try. What it is not built for is writing. Reflection in Daylio is an optional note behind a tap, and the app's energy is gamified rather than calm. People who want to sit with a feeling for a minute tend to find it thin; people who would never write a paragraph anyway tend to love it. Where Luvia differs: writing is first class, the page saves itself as you go, everything stays on your device, and the reward is calm rather than a streak.

Bearable: for health detective work

Bearable is a different tool wearing the same category. It tracks mood alongside symptoms, medication, sleep, energy and dozens of custom factors, then looks for correlations, which makes it the strongest choice of the three if you are managing a chronic condition, adjusting medication, or trying to show a doctor a pattern. That power has a cost: the interface is dense, logging takes longer, and the app treats you a little like a study with one participant. If that sentence sounds appealing, Bearable is probably your answer. If it sounds exhausting, that is the gap Luvia sits in: five soft mood tones instead of a questionnaire, a journal instead of a report, and rituals you can hold in a minute.

Luvia: privacy, rituals, and one human feature

Luvia is built around a different belief: that checking in should feel like exhaling, not reporting. Mood check-ins use five weather-like tones instead of scores, the journal saves itself while you write and keeps everything on your device, and there are no ads, no streaks and no tracking. Around that core sit the rituals: a plain meditation timer, the research-backed WOOP method for goals, and sealed notes to your future self. And there is one thing the other two have no equivalent for: Kind Messages, anonymous moderated notes of support between people, one message and one reply, never a chat. The honest trade-offs: Luvia is iOS only, and it is subscription only with no free tier, because the subscription pays for the human and automated moderation that keeps Kind Messages safe.

The short version

Choose Luvia if you want your reflections private and on your device, a calmer and quieter app, writing that saves itself, rituals with real research behind them, and a moment of anonymous human kindness, which is the feature the rest of the category simply does not have. Choose Daylio if you want the longest record for the least effort and enjoy stats. Choose Bearable if mood is one signal among many in a health picture you are actively investigating. None of these is the wrong answer; they are answers to different questions.
