The wish: how to pick a goal WOOP can actually move.

WOOP asks for a wish before anything else, and most of the ones that fail were lost right there. Here is how to choose one the method can hold.

If you have read the introduction to WOOP, you know the shape of it: wish, outcome, obstacle, plan. What the overview could not do was slow down on the first word. This post does that, because in practice the wish is where most WOOPs quietly fail. Not at the plan, not at the obstacle, but in the first ten seconds, when someone writes something that sounds like a goal and is actually a mood.

Where WOOPs go wrong

The usual first attempt looks like one of these: be happier, get fit, be more present with the kids, stop procrastinating. They are honest, and they are the reason people want to change, which is exactly why they make poor wishes. A wish in WOOP has one job. It has to be something you can picture coming true, something an obstacle can block, and something a plan can protect. Be happier cannot be pictured as a single scene. Nothing specific blocks it. No if-then sentence protects it. The method has nothing to grip, and the later steps turn vague to match.

A wish is not the thing you want most. It is the part of it you could move in the next few weeks.

The three tests

Gabriele Oettingen is unusually precise about what counts. In her lab's instructions and in Rethinking Positive Thinking, a wish should be challenging but feasible, set within a defined window, and within your own control. Each test rules out a familiar failure.

Challenging but feasible. This is the heart of mental contrasting. In the 2001 studies that first tested the method, contrasting a wished-for future with its obstacle raised commitment only when people already judged the wish attainable. When they judged it out of reach, contrasting lowered commitment instead, which the researchers treated as a feature: the method helps you let go of wishes that are not live. So if you write a wish you privately rate at one in ten, WOOP will do its job and talk you out of it. Pick one you would rate at six or seven. Hard enough to matter, close enough to believe.

Within about four weeks. The window is not arbitrary. It keeps the wish small enough to picture concretely and close enough that the obstacle you name is the one you will actually meet. A wish for the year is a direction. A wish for the month is a thing you can do on Thursday.

In your control. Get the promotion is not yours to give. Prepare and ask for the conversation is. Have my partner help more is theirs. Say clearly what I need by Friday is yours. The test is simple: if everyone else behaves exactly as they do now, can you still fulfil it?

One wish, reshaped

Take someone whose first draft is be healthier. Run the tests and it fails all three: it has no edge to be challenging against, no window, and no single act. So they narrow it. Exercise more is closer, but more than what, and by when. Walk twenty minutes before work, four days this week passes. It is challenging, because mornings are already full. It is feasible, because twenty minutes exists if something else gives. It has a window. And no one else needs to agree.

Notice what happened to the original wish. It did not disappear. Being healthier is still the reason, and it will show up again in the outcome step as the feeling of arriving at work clear-headed. What changed is that the wish is now something a morning can either contain or not, which means the obstacle can be found and the plan can be written. We will carry this same wish through the obstacle post, because the quality of the wish sets the ceiling for everything after it.

Be healthier is why. Walk before work is what.

Feelings and behaviours

A great many wishes are about how we want to feel: calmer, less anxious, more confident. These are real, and it would be unkind to say they do not belong. But feelings are outcomes, not wishes, and WOOP already has a place for them. The trick is to ask what you would be doing, in a given week, if the feeling were already here. Calmer often turns out to mean stop checking email after nine. More confident often means speak first in Monday's meeting. The behaviour becomes the wish; the feeling moves to the outcome, where it can be pictured as vividly as you like.

This matters for a practical reason. Oettingen and Mayer's 2002 studies found that positive fantasies about a future, enjoyed on their own, predicted less effort and worse results over time. A wish made of pure feeling invites exactly that kind of fantasy. A wish made of behaviour gives the feeling something to be attached to.

When a wish is someone else's

There is one more test, and it is quieter than the others. Some wishes we carry are not ours. They were a parent's hope, a partner's request, a standard absorbed from somewhere and never examined. These fail the feasibility test in a particular way: you can picture the outcome, but when you try to feel it fully, nothing arrives. That flatness is information. Oettingen's own reading of mental contrasting is that it sorts wishes by how much you actually expect and want them, and a wish you do not want will not survive the contrast. If the outcome step keeps coming up empty, the honest move is to put that wish down and pick one that produces a pull when you imagine it. You can do this without deciding anything about the people who gave it to you.

The first field

When you open a WOOP in Luvia's rituals, the wish is the first field, and it is deliberately small. There is room for one sentence, not a paragraph, and the next step will not take a feeling in place of an act. That constraint is the point. Before you type, run the three tests: would I rate it six or seven out of ten, could it happen in the next few weeks, and is it mine to do. If it passes, write it as plainly as you would say it aloud. If it does not, narrow it until it does. Everything WOOP can do for you begins with that one line.

The short answers

What is a good WOOP wish example?

Something specific, time-bound and within your control, such as walk twenty minutes before work four days this week, or send the three overdue replies by Wednesday. Be healthier and be more productive are reasons, not wishes, and they need narrowing first.

How hard should a WOOP wish be?

Challenging but feasible. Oettingen's research found that mental contrasting raises commitment when you already judge the wish attainable and lowers it when you do not. Aim for a wish you would rate at around six or seven out of ten.

Can a WOOP wish be about a feeling?

Feelings belong in the outcome step, not the wish. Ask what you would be doing in a typical week if the feeling were already there, make that behaviour the wish, and let the feeling be the outcome you picture.

How far ahead should a WOOP wish be set?

About four weeks or less. A short window keeps the wish concrete, keeps the obstacle realistic, and makes it easy to tell whether it came true.
